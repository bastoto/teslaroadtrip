Day-by-day · summer rhythm

Hour-by-hour, mobile-friendly.

Built around 9 am wake / 9 pm sleep. Mountain roads average 50–60 km/h, and alpine elevation gain reduces EV range by ~20–25%, so each day has 1 charging stop sized for a ~225 km/charge car. Tap any bolded place for photos.

01

Aravis massif · Col des Aravis

Skip the famous lakes. Drive straight up into the Aravis massif — Reblochon cheese country, alpine pastures, and the Col des Aravis (1,486 m) with a panoramic view of Mont Blanc directly across the valley. Sleep on the pass.

Field manual

What you actually need to know.

01

Pass openings

  • Col de l'Iseran: open early-July to late-September only · check inforoute73.fr before D4
  • Cormet de Roselend: open mid-May to October
  • Lac du Mont-Cenis: open year-round but pass road only May–November
  • If Iseran is closed: alternative is Col du Mont-Cenis (2,083 m, lower but also dramatic)
02

EV range in the mountains

  • Mountain driving with elevation gain consumes ~20–25% more battery per km
  • Charge to 90% before each mountain pass section (Cormet, Iseran)
  • Regen on descents recovers ~30% of climbing energy · use it
  • Pre-condition battery if driving above 2,000 m for >30 min in cold mornings
03

Sleeping legality (France)

  • Sleeping inside a parked car is legal almost everywhere · camping (chairs out) is what's banned
  • Most alpine D-roads have signed lay-bys with no overnight ban
  • Avoid signposted "interdit aux camping-cars" zones near busy resorts (Val d'Isère in season)
  • Park4Night has dense coverage in this region · check reviews before each stop
04

Budget anchors

  • Charging: ~€80. 470 km in mountains = ~570 km flat-equivalent. ~3 charge stops × €16.
  • Tolls: €0 · all D-roads + free A43 sections
  • Bike rental Annecy: €15
  • Vanoise NP entrance: free (national park)
  • Food: ~€80 (cook + 2 mountain meals)
  • Total ~€175 · 5 days

Three lakes, three passes, no rush.

Roselend is glacial blue, Iseran is roof-of-Europe, Bonneval is a postcard. The trip is built so you spend most afternoons by water and most evenings above 1,500 m.
